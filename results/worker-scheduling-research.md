# Throughput-first worker scheduling

Research snapshot for the Linux epoll pilot at `8750db41`. The objective is the highest sustained rate of correct responses that meets the workload's latency and error targets. CPU time and memory describe the cost. Effective CPU availability, cgroup throttling, overload behavior and bounded resources constrain the design.

**First test workers selected from the CPU budget and ready before the first connection.** Compare that policy with a prestarted standby and the existing cold-start controller. Keep connection placement and handler dispatch as separate experiments. The preopened burst shows that provisioning a thread and putting the work on it are distinct decisions. [Pilot #204](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/pull/204) · [Benchmark plan #206](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/206)

## What ktServe schedules today

- **Socket I/O.** Each epoll loop has an owning OS thread in the same process. Linux decides readiness and which runnable thread gets CPU; ktServe owns socket placement, per-connection HTTP state, buffers and ordering. The calling thread runs loop zero. With explicit multiworker serving, `kotlinx.coroutines` launches the other loops on a dedicated fixed thread pool. The library's default is one loop. [Linux API](../components/ktserve/src/nativeMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/LinuxServer.kt) · [Fixed worker lifecycle](../components/ktserve/src/nativeMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/EpollWorkers.kt) · [Kotlin dispatcher](https://kotlinlang.org/api/kotlinx.coroutines/kotlinx-coroutines-core/kotlinx.coroutines/new-fixed-thread-pool-context.html)
- **Worker count.** ktServe's optional benchmark controller checks the calling thread's affinity and the visible cgroup v2 hierarchy at startup. It creates worker two after three samples each at least one second long with primary thread CPU at or above 75%. Five samples below 30% on both loops initiate retirement. The secondary pauses accepts and drains naturally as its established peers close. A primary event-loop iteration drives the controller, so a stalled primary also delays detection. [CPU budget](../components/ktserve/src/nativeMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/WorkerCpuBudget.kt) · [Controller](../components/ktserve/src/nativeMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/AdaptiveEpollWorkers.kt)
- **Connection placement.** The adaptive pilot uses a shared listener with an admission budget shared by both loops. The optional handoff takes one batch of half of the recently active, eligible connections. Each moves at a clean HTTP exchange boundary with its original TCP descriptor, idle age and admission slot. Eventfd wakes the receiving loop. A connection with partial input or an active exchange stays on its owner. [Handoff](../components/ktserve/src/nativeMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/EpollKeepAliveHandoff.kt) · [Transport and ownership](../components/ktserve/TRANSPORT.md)
- **Application work.** Plain synchronous routes such as `/json` run inline on the owner. Suspended handlers use coroutines on `Dispatchers.Default`. Explicit blocking handlers use `Dispatchers.IO` behind a semaphore per loop. The coroutine runtime schedules these dispatched jobs. ktServe decides when to launch its epoll workers and which loop owns a socket. [HTTP loop](../components/ktserve/src/commonMain/kotlin/codes/draeger/ktserve/HttpServer.kt) · [Kotlin dispatcher roles](https://github.com/Kotlin/kotlinx.coroutines/blob/1.11.0/kotlinx-coroutines-core/common/src/CoroutineDispatcher.kt)

## What the measurements establish

The follow-up CI used the same release binary on each architecture, separate two-vCPU affinity masks for the server and generator, and a 12-second `/json` burst on 64 TCP connections that had already completed an HTTP exchange. All 24 bursts and all 120 trial phases passed the harness checks. Each table value is the median of three per-trial rates. The underlying Linux VMs were GitHub-hosted and shared. [Run 45669558680 and raw data](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/actions/runs/45669558680)

| Linux | Window | Fixed 1 req/s | Auto req/s | Auto with handoff req/s | Fixed 2 req/s |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| x86_64 | Full burst | 114,298 | 114,768 | 138,086 | 151,839 |
| x86_64 | After the first 5 s | 115,556 | 115,086 | 152,034 | 151,335 |
| ARM64 | Full burst | 101,533 | 100,177 | 150,350 | 163,564 |
| ARM64 | After the first 5 s | 102,010 | 101,310 | 173,202 | 165,460 |

Every handoff trial moved 32 sockets and served all 64 original peers through the burst. Auto without handoff reached 64/0 despite starting the second loop. The raw files give a handoff time of roughly 3.67–3.70 seconds from the start of timed traffic, using server elapsed time and the client's recorded phase start and preopen setup. The first three whole seconds of the handoff mode tracked fixed one. After five seconds handoff was about 32% above fixed one on x86_64 and 70% above it on ARM64. Over the full burst it was 9.1% and 8.1% below fixed two. The first mechanism recovered access to a second core and the delay reduced the cumulative win. Fixed two gave its less populated loop 19–29 sockets, so its rate is an observed baseline with some placement skew. Exact balance and steady-state gains require their own repeats. [CI phase and per-socket artifacts](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/actions/runs/45669558680)

The retirement path can block the primary during dispatcher cleanup. A separately instrumented ARM64 run spent 82 ms inside the close call, which lined up with a latency spike at 800 requests/s. Two hosted handoff trials saw quiet-phase p99 outliers of 5.9 ms and 7.8 ms. Correctness at activation and acceptable latency at retirement are separate gates. [Retirement evidence #205](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/205)

Two earlier results constrain the upside. `SO_REUSEPORT` substantially evened request counts while peak goodput changed by only −0.85% to +1.77% across six cells. Four workers lost throughput in three of four earlier ARM64 cells. Moving each small JSON handler to a dispatcher with eventfd also decreased peak throughput from 142,798 to 106,857 req/s on x86_64 and from 95,978 to 71,934 on ARM64. More even placement, more threads and more coroutine dispatch each have different costs. The matched two-CPU comparison with Axum put fixed-two ktServe at about 91–92% of Axum's `/json` rate in those exploratory cells. That already used the available server CPUs. Further sustained gains at that budget need better per-core service time or less contention. [Listener measurements](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/actions/runs/45353312293) · [Four-worker evidence](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/87) · [Handler measurements](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/actions/runs/45354961631) · [Matched CPU budget #116](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/116#issuecomment-7129968569)

## What established runtimes and research suggest

| System or study | Mechanism | Testable implication for ktServe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Axum and Tokio | Axum spawns a task per accepted connection. Tokio's multi-thread runtime starts a fixed count of async workers and schedules runnable tasks using local queues, a global queue and work stealing. An async task can run on a different thread after yielding. | Use local queues and occasional stealing as a comparison for transferable application work. Keep socket ownership, response order and I/O wakeups explicit. [Axum accept path](https://github.com/tokio-rs/axum/blob/axum-v0.8.9/axum/src/serve/mod.rs#L389-L403) · [Tokio scheduler](https://github.com/tokio-rs/tokio/blob/tokio-1.54.1/tokio/src/runtime/mod.rs#L345-L382) |
| Netty and Micronaut | Netty's default chooser assigns accepted channels round-robin to long-lived, owning event loops. Netty 4.2 also has an optional autoscaling chooser with a separate monitor, per-loop utilization, consecutive-window thresholds and a zero-channel requirement for suspension. That chooser assigns new registrations to the active group. The repository's Micronaut fixture uses the ordinary Netty group. | Compare round-robin admission with the shared listener and use a separate monitoring path. Long-lived channels can delay retirement. Micronaut's measured multiworker gain supplies no evidence for adaptive worker counts or AOT as its sole cause. | Go HTTP and runtime | Go starts a goroutine per accepted connection. Its scheduler can steal runnable goroutines and the Linux netpoller wakes goroutines after socket events. The default `GOMAXPROCS` can track affinity and cgroup changes independently of HTTP request pressure. | Treat CPU-budget discovery separately from request redistribution. The goroutine and poller architecture is a different reference design. | Linux, NGINX and HAProxy | `EPOLLEXCLUSIVE` reduces wakeups and allows one or more registered pollers to wake. NGINX rotates listener registrations to mitigate preference for the earliest one. The Linux `tcp_migrate_req` option concerns listener-side pending sockets, including established sockets in the accept queue. ktServe's already accepted TCP sockets need a separate handoff. ## Experiment order

1. **Measure the capacity curve and take lifecycle work out of the I/O loop.** At a two-CPU budget compare fixed 1, fixed 2 and the existing Auto with handoff. Factor the startup policy as automatic budget-selected workers active before listen versus a prestarted secondary parked on a blocking wait. The eager variant should reproduce the equivalent fixed configuration. Compare cycle latency and isolation after fixing dispatcher retirement in #205. Keep the coroutine implementation as the first candidate. A custom thread primitive only has a case if profiling shows Kotlin lifecycle or wake overhead stays on the critical path. [Retirement issue](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/205) · [Comparison issue](https://github.com/christian-draeger/ktserve/issues/206)
2. **Measure fast activation against the eager baseline.** Start with periodic owner-local snapshots of full readiness batches, age of known pending ready work, per-loop completed exchanges and a timer's lateness. A full epoll batch is a proxy and does not expose the exact age of kernel events. Wake a parked worker only when ready work waits persistently and another viable CPU or owner can help. Track CPU time, affinity, cgroup `cpu.stat` and pressure as capacity guards. A database or upstream wait does not itself signal ready work. Perform slow control sampling and thread cleanup off the data path, batch cross-thread signaling, and keep scale-up and park thresholds asymmetric. Include a throughput-first mode that stays ready after the first burst. [cgroup v2 CPU controller](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#cpu-interface-files) · [Linux pressure data](https://docs.kernel.org/accounting/psi.html)
3. **Improve placement only once a skew test shows a capacity gap.** Compare shared-listener batch/rotation changes and existing reuse-port with round-robin handoff from one acceptor. Measure acceptor contention before adding two-choice, pressure-aware placement. Wake the target only on a queue transition or coalesced batch. Established hot peers still need a bounded, clean-boundary move with one owner and one admission lease. Require a sustained donor/receiver imbalance and cap moves per period. Measure the cost of migrations and whether requests, rather than just socket counts, were balanced. A single non-pipelined HTTP/1.1 connection has one exchange in flight. Pipelining or HTTP/2 changes the scheduling unit and has protocol-specific ordering constraints. [HTTP/1.1 response ordering](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9112#section-9.3.2)
4. **Isolate costly handlers as a separate path.** Sweep short, CPU-heavy, variable-duration and suspending work in the same application. Compare inline, bounded coroutine dispatch and batched completion. Measure enqueue-to-start, application cost, response-ready-to-owner and rate of short operations while heavy requests run. Keep fast handlers inline unless the mixed-workload result shows a clear benefit. Investigate work stealing only for jobs whose measured cost exceeds the queue and wake overhead, within one shared CPU and overload budget. Test the transport-independent policy before considering JVM or kqueue adapters.
5. **Expand cores and transports only with headroom and profile evidence.** Run fixed 4 and 8 before adding those counts to a controller. Record physical cores, SMT siblings, NUMA, affinity, CPU quota and generator headroom. The current public Linux runner class has four vCPUs, enough for two server and two generator vCPUs in this setup. It cannot supply four isolated server vCPUs plus a generator on that VM. Keep higher-core and true NIC RSS/IRQ steering tests scoped to environments that can measure them. Profile parser allocation, serialization, GC and network work when throughput stops tracking worker count. [Runner resources](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/runners/github-hosted-runners#standard-github-hosted-runners-for-public-repositories) · [Linux RSS and CPU steering](https://docs.kernel.org/networking/scaling.html)

## Decision gates

- **Measure the useful work.** Use the existing preopened closed-loop harness to measure peak rates and continuous socket identity. Add open-loop k6 arrival-rate levels for matched-load capacity and per-route latency. Track successfully validated responses and offered work. Report generator drops, throttling, timeouts, request errors and reconnects alongside measured throughput. Use the existing application-mix and ingress SLOs for production-shaped traffic. [Benchmark protocol](../BENCHMARK_PROTOCOL.md) · [k6 load models](https://grafana.com/docs/k6/latest/using-k6/scenarios/concepts/open-vs-closed/)
- **Exercise transitions.** Include sub-second and multi-second bursts, 12-second bursts, sustained ramps, repeated load/quiet cycles and quiet after retirement. Cover identical hot keep-alives, very few hot peers among many idle peers, TCP churn, fragment and streaming cases, and a slow dependency control. Record time from offered demand to 90% of the best validated fixed capacity, per-window p99 and p99.9 with observation counts, route mix and worker imbalance. Record every activation, retirement and handoff in timed diagnostic traces.
- **Bound resources and preserve wire behavior.** Check zero unexpected reconnects or exchange errors in valid load cells, stable thread/socket/FD counts after repeated cycles, listener admissions at the global limit, clean cancellation and shutdown, and no accidental owner overlap. Account explicitly for slowdown or rejection at overload. Validate concurrent invocation of shared inline handlers and observers before changing the public default.
- **Promote only against the best relevant fixed comparator.** A proposed target for confirmation is at least 95% of its sustained and full long-burst goodput while meeting the existing latency, error and continuity gates. Report short bursts separately and predeclare the crossover or wake target. Interleave modes with identical settings, use at least five paired confirmation repetitions, show dispersion and keep negative results. Shared-runner measurements select candidates. Release-level claims still follow the controlled benchmark protocol.

The next decision is whether a throughput-first startup policy matches the best fixed capacity with enough benefit to justify a dynamic standby. Once both loops are already fed and saturated, further peak throughput comes from scaling the validated core count or lowering service cost.
